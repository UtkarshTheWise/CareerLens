# CareerLens should win on proof, not polish

CareerLens is entering a crowded market, but the crowding sits almost entirely on the half of the idea that matters least. Resume scoring against a job description, Chrome extensions that read job pages, tailored "ATS" resumes and application trackers are commodities in 2026: Teal, Simplify, Huntr, Careerflow, Jobscan and Jobright all ship versions of them, often free. **None of the mainstream job-seeker tools reviewed checks whether a claimed skill is backed by real work**, and Indian placement platforms such as Superset and HCL GUVI's PlacementPreparation.io measure readiness through tests, not proof of work. The closest analogues are HackerRank's open-source MIT "hiring-agent" (resume plus GitHub scoring, about 7.1k stars), the startup GradPipe, and hackathon projects like RashDev, so "AI resume + GitHub score" on its own is not novel and judges may have seen it. What CareerLens can defend is the combination: per-claim evidence links, an explainable sub-score breakdown, design-portfolio evidence, and cohort analytics for placement cells. A zero-cost prototype is feasible on Vercel Hobby, Supabase Free, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite or 3.8 Flash with Groq as fallback, and authenticated GitHub GraphQL. The traps are new: Gemini 2.5 is closed to new projects, free Gemini quotas are no longer published, Cerebras and Koyeb dropped their free tiers, and LinkedIn and Behance data cannot be obtained through APIs. For the two-agent build, a frozen OpenAPI contract, one shared AGENTS.md, exclusive directory ownership enforced by Claude Code permission rules, and a plan-mode integration pass by Claude Code are the practical controls. One budget warning applies: Codex CLI and IDE access currently requires ChatGPT Plus or an API key.

## The extension half of the idea is already free elsewhere

Job-seeker tools fall into four clusters: ATS keyword scorers (Jobscan, Rezi, Resume Worded), builders with AI writers (Kickresume, Enhancv, Teal), extension-tracker-autofill copilots (Simplify, Huntr, Careerflow, Jobright, Teal) and volume auto-apply services (LazyApply, LoopCV, AIApply). The team's Chrome extension idea (scrape the job description, tailor an ATS resume, track applications) lands squarely in the third cluster, which is the most crowded and most generous. Simplify's Copilot autofills on **100+ portals including Workday, Greenhouse and Lever**, scores the resume against the job description, and Simplify publicly commits to keeping "autofill, application tracker, job matches, and basic resume builder" free ([Simplify](https://simplify.jobs/copilot)). Teal's free tier gives **unlimited resumes and unlimited job tracking** but shows only the top 5 job-description keywords ([Teal pricing](https://www.tealhq.com/pricing)). Huntr's free plan tracks up to 100 jobs and includes a Chrome job clipper and basic matching ([Huntr pricing](https://huntr.co/pricing)). Careerflow's FAQ says its tracker has no limit on the number of jobs ([Careerflow FAQ](https://www.careerflow.ai/faq)).

Every score in this table is keyword- or skill-list-based, and user complaints follow from that. On r/resumes, users warn that recruiters are "turned off by 'keyword stuffing'" when people chase a Jobscan score ([Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/resumes/comments/1fd2jsf/is_this_really_how_jobscan_is_supposed_to_work/)). The AI rewriters also make the inflation problem worse: one review found Jobright's tailoring inventing metrics like "reducing latency by 67%" ([ATSVerification](https://atsverification.com/blog/jobright-ai-review-2026/)), and even a competitor concedes that Rezi's AI "can generate generic or inflated content if unchecked" ([Enhancv](https://enhancv.com/blog/enhancv-vs-rezi/)). Jobright, the most aggressive matcher, is effectively US-only, with coverage in India described as "sparse to nonexistent" ([ATSVerification](https://atsverification.com/blog/jobright-ai-review-2026/)). Autofill is unreliable even for the market leader. A competitor's own testing puts Simplify at roughly 70% on Workday and 40% on Taleo ([JobHire.ai](https://jobhire.ai/blog/simplify-jobs-review)); those numbers are unverified and come from a rival.

The implication is blunt. A match score in a side panel is the minimum a judge will expect, not a differentiator. If the team builds the extension, its job should be to deliver an evidence-weighted match ("72% keyword match, but only 40% of those skills are backed by your repos"), not to compete on autofill or tracking. Tailored resume generation should be constrained to claims the evidence supports and should flag numbers nothing in the evidence backs. That turns the category's main weakness, fabricated content, into CareerLens's selling point.

## Proof-of-work scoring exists, but not for students, designers or placement cells

Evidence-based verification is not empty territory, and the team should say so before a judge does. HackerRank's MIT-licensed **hiring-agent** "extracts structured data from resume PDFs, enriches candidates with GitHub signals, and produces fair, explainable scoring evaluations." It applies author-commit thresholds to confirm real participation, runs an LLM originality check, selects exactly seven qualifying projects, and outputs a score with explicit deductions and bonuses, using Gemini or local Ollama models ([GitHub: interviewstreet/hiring-agent](https://github.com/interviewstreet/hiring-agent)). GradPipe, a 2025 startup, combines resume parsing with analysis of up to 10,000 commits into a 0–100 "ShowOFF Score" across 50+ dimensions, and is free for developers ([EveryDev.ai](https://www.everydev.ai/tools/gradpipe)). GitRoll scores GitHub profiles for recruiters ([GitRoll](https://gitroll.io/)). At hackathon level, RashDev (FOSS Hack 2026) builds a credibility score from GitHub and Codeforces with roadmaps ([FOSS United](https://fossunited.org/hack/fosshack26/p/fckh0ajbou)), and a Devpost project called "Verify" cross-references GitHub repositories "to validate the authenticity of resume" claims ([Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/verify-27gui6)). The first wave of GitHub-as-resume products largely failed or was absorbed. OpenSauced joined the Linux Foundation in October 2024 ([Plushcap](https://www.plushcap.com/companies/opensauced)), CodersRank's About page was last updated in 2022 and shows placeholder stats ([CodersRank](https://codersrank.io/about-us/)), and Andela bought the assessment company Woven in January 2026 ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/woven)). The pattern suggests standalone developer scores struggle as consumer businesses and survive inside institutional or hiring products.

The long-term threat is LinkedIn. In January 2026 it launched verified AI-skill certificates issued by partner apps (Replit, Lovable, Descript, Relay.app), with **GitHub named as a partner "in the coming months"** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/28/linkedin-will-let-you-show-off-your-vibe-coding-chops-with-a-certificate/)). Those certificates verify use of a specific tool, though. They do not check the claims on a resume against a job description, and whether GitHub actually went live as a partner is unconfirmed.

In India, the placement-cell market is test-centric. Superset (owned by Great Learning) sells a placement ERP with funnel analytics, "ATS-style resume analysis per student" and **"batch-level readiness insights"**. Its "verification" means departmental checks of grades and enrolment, not checks of skills against code ([Superset](https://joinsuperset.com/university.html)). HCL GUVI's PlacementPreparation.io sells colleges an AI "Placement Readiness Index", batch dashboards and at-risk flags built on mock tests patterned on TCS, Wipro and Accenture, and names SRM IST among its partners ([PlacementPreparation.io](https://www.placementpreparation.io/for-colleges/)). Mettl, Unstop, Naukri Campus and Internshala likewise measure readiness through assessments, competitions or training completion ([Mettl](https://mettl.com/campus-hiring/); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/spotlight/news-wire/the-rise-of-access-pr-how-unstop-is-changing-the-way-companies-discover-early-talent); [Internshala](https://internshala.com/blog/internshala-trainings-for-your-college/)). Batch dashboards are therefore not a differentiator on their own. A proof-of-work dimension inside those dashboards would be. The macro data supports this: India's national employability rose to **56.35% in 2026**, and project-based hiring is up 38% year on year ([Careers360 on India Skills Report 2026](https://news.careers360.com/india-skills-report-2026-employability-56-35-pc-ai-tools-digital-gig-economy-workforce-global-talent-hub)).

CareerLens is genuinely different on four points, and the pitch should name them. First, it verifies per claim: each resume skill links to the repo, commit range or design artifact that proves it, or is marked unsupported. Hiring-agent scores categories but does not explicitly cross-reference individual claims. Second, it accepts multi-modal evidence. No competitor found covers Behance, Dribbble or Figma portfolios, and no published research on verifying design portfolios turned up either, so design and product roles are open ground. Third, it is student-facing and diagnostic, turning the gap into a roadmap. Fourth, its cohort view tells a training and placement officer whether a batch's claimed skills are backed by work they actually built, which Superset and GUVI do not offer. The line for the judges: existing tools tell a placement officer how students perform on tests, and CareerLens tells them whether what students claim is backed by real work.

The explainable score should be built from known, published signals rather than a black box. Candidates include hygiene (only 0.5% of GitHub repos have a README, licence, homepage, README images and description together, per [UW–Madison OSPO](https://ospo.wisc.edu/blog/2024/repo-quality-metrics)), engineering practice modelled on OpenSSF Scorecard checks such as CI-Tests, Code-Review and Maintained ([ossf/scorecard](https://github.com/ossf/scorecard)), commit consistency measured as the coefficient of variation with a stability threshold of 0.5 ([arXiv 2508.02487](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02487v2)), authorship share, and originality via Dolos-style winnowing fingerprints against popular tutorial templates ([Dolos, arXiv 2402.10853](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.10853v2)). Tutorial-clone detection has no off-the-shelf tool, so the team would have to build it. CareerLens should **not** advertise "AI-written resume detection." Commercial detectors scored only 25–56% on a 2026 resume corpus, against 95% for a purpose-built stylometric classifier ([LREC 2026](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-lrec/2026.lrec-main.581/)), and a Stanford study found GPT detectors misclassified over half of essays by non-native English speakers ([ResumeGeni](https://resumegeni.com/blog/ai-detection-tools-recruiters-use-2026)). That bias would land directly on Indian students. Checking claims against evidence is fairer, and it is easier to defend in front of judges.

The honest uncertainty is whether any of this predicts hiring outcomes. No published study validating a repo-quality score against hiring results for new graduates was found, and no study showing that ATS match scores predict interviews was found either. Present the score as diagnostic, not predictive.

## Free LLM quotas shrank in 2026; build for fallback from day one

The LLM layer changed the most in 2026, and tutorials from 2025 will mislead the team. **Google restricted Gemini 2.5 models on 18 September 2026 to users who had actively used them**, directing new projects to Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite or 3.8 Flash. Both remain "Free of charge" on the pricing page, and `temperature`, `top_p` and `top_k` were deprecated for the latest models in July ([Gemini changelog](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/changelog); [Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). Google no longer publishes free-tier quotas. The official page says to look them up in AI Studio ([Gemini rate limits](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits)), and one aggregator estimates **about 20 requests/day for 3.x Flash and about 500/day for Flash-Lite** ([PricePerToken](https://pricepertoken.com/endpoints/google-ai-studio/free)). Older figures of 1,500 requests/day are stale. Quotas apply per project, not per key, and reset at midnight Pacific, which is 12:30 or 13:30 IST ([YingTu](https://yingtu.ai/en/blog/google-gemini-api-free-tier-limits-2026)). Structured output works with a JSON Schema passed from Pydantic or Zod, but "very large or deeply nested schemas may be rejected" ([Gemini structured output](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/structured-output)). That argues for several small per-step schemas over one giant report schema.

Two Gemini terms affect a student-data product directly. Free-tier content is "used to improve our products," and the terms say "Do not submit sensitive, confidential, or personal information to the Unpaid Services." The API also requires users to be **18 or older** ([Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)). The demo should use synthetic or consenting resumes with contact details stripped before any LLM call, and an adult teammate should own the key.

Groq is the best fallback. On the free plan, `openai/gpt-oss-120b`, `gpt-oss-20b` and `qwen3.8-27b` each get **30 RPM, 1,000 requests/day, 8K tokens/minute and 200K tokens/day**, and support strict `json_schema` decoding ([Groq rate limits](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits); [Groq structured outputs](https://console.groq.com/docs/structured-outputs)). Two gotchas apply: streaming does not work with structured outputs, and Llama 3.x models no longer appear on the free plan, so code that uses `llama-3.3-70b-versatile` may break. The 8K TPM cap also means long resumes plus repo summaries need chunking. Other options have narrowed. Cerebras dropped its permanent free tier on 16 July 2026 for a card-gated $5 trial ([AgentDeals](https://agentdeals.dev/vendor/cerebras)). OpenRouter's `:free` models allow only **50 requests/day** without a $10 credit purchase ([OpenRouter limits](https://openrouter.ai/docs/api-reference/limits)). GitHub Models gives 50–150 requests/day depending on model tier ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/github-models/use-github-models/prototyping-with-ai-models)). Ollama supports JSON-schema outputs locally but not on Ollama Cloud ([Ollama docs](https://docs.ollama.com/capabilities/structured-outputs)), which makes it a sensible offline backup for demo day. The architectural answer is a single `generateStructured(schema, prompt)` wrapper that tries Gemini, catches 429s, falls back to Groq, and caches every result in Postgres.

Embeddings are optional at hackathon scale. Sources disagree on whether `gemini-embedding-2` has a free tier ([AgentDeals](https://agentdeals.dev/vendor/google-gemini-embedding-2) vs. [YingTu](https://yingtu.ai/en/blog/google-gemini-api-free-tier-limits-2026)), so the safer choices are a small local model or GitHub Models embeddings (150 requests/day), stored in Supabase pgvector ([Supabase pgvector](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/extensions/pgvector)). A skill-taxonomy overlap plus an LLM judgement may be enough on its own.

## GitHub is open, LinkedIn and Behance are closed, and hosting has new limits

| Extension | MV3 `sidePanel` + JobPosting JSON-LD first | `sidePanel.open()` requires a user gesture ([Chrome docs](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/api/sidePanel)) |

The hosting landscape has thinned. Koyeb ended its free tier for new users in February 2026, and Fly.io offers only a short trial ([AgentDeals](https://agentdeals.dev/hosting-free-tier-comparison-2026)). Railway gives a one-time $5 credit and then $1/month ([Railway](https://docs.railway.com/reference/pricing/free-trial)). Hugging Face now requires a paid plan for Docker Spaces ([HF Spaces](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/spaces-overview)). Render's free Postgres expires after 30 days ([Render](https://render.com/docs/free)). Cloudflare Workers' 10 ms CPU limit rules out PDF work there ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)). Three Vercel details will bite a student team. A repo created under a GitHub organization cannot deploy on Hobby, so deploy from a personal fork or personal repo. Resume uploads must go directly from the browser to Supabase Storage through a signed URL, with only the path sent to the function, to stay under the 4.5 MB limit. Functions default to US East. Keeping Supabase alive before judging is easy: open the dashboard or have a free GitHub Actions cron hit the API daily ([Supabase pausing](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/free-project-pausing.md)).

GitHub is the only evidence source with a usable free API, and it needs authentication. Unauthenticated REST allows only **60 requests/hour per IP**, which a hackathon venue sharing one NAT address would exhaust in minutes. With a token the limit is 5,000/hour ([GitHub REST limits](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/using-the-rest-api/rate-limits-for-the-rest-api)). One GraphQL query can pull the contribution calendar, non-fork owned repos and per-repo language sizes. The repo statistics endpoints return 202 while GitHub computes them, so the client must retry ([GitHub statistics](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/metrics/statistics)). Signing in with Supabase Auth's GitHub provider yields the user's token as a side effect.

Every other evidence source is restricted. LinkedIn's self-serve API returns only name, headline, photo and email; positions, skills and education require partner programs ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/shared/authentication/getting-access)). The hiQ v. LinkedIn litigation ended in a $500,000 judgment and a permanent injunction against scraping ([Privacy World](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2022/12/linkedins-data-scraping-battle-with-hiq-labs-ends-with-proposed-judgment/)). The defensible design is for the user to upload LinkedIn's own profile PDF export or paste profile text. An opt-in, per-page, user-clicked read in the extension is still a terms-of-service grey area. Adobe has said the Behance API "is unavailable," and developers still could not get keys as of 2024 ([Adobe community](https://experienceleaguecommunities.adobe.com/adobe-developer-20/where-can-i-find-behance-api-144990)). Dribbble's API returns only the authenticated user's own shots ([Dribbble API](https://developer.dribbble.com/v2/)). Since November 2025, Figma caps View and Collab seats at **20 Tier-1 requests per month** ([Figma rate limits](https://developers.figma.com/docs/rest-api/rate-limits/)). Design evidence should therefore be link-based: the user supplies portfolio URLs and project descriptions, CareerLens reads public Open Graph metadata, and the LLM judges the description against the claimed skill. That is weaker evidence than code, and the score should label it as such.

For job pages, the extension should first parse schema.org `JobPosting` JSON-LD, which requires `title`, `description`, `datePosted` and `hiringOrganization` ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/job-posting)). It should fall back to per-site DOM adapters and finally to page text plus LLM extraction. Whether each major ATS currently embeds JSON-LD was not verified, so the fallbacks are necessary. Loading the extension unpacked costs nothing; the Chrome Web Store registration fee was not verified.

## One contract, two owners, and a third pass that only integrates

The main risk in pairing Claude Code and Codex is semantic, not textual: the agents drift from the contract, duplicate types, and "fix" each other's code. A practitioner write-up cites a 27.67% conflict rate across AI-generated pull requests and a 30% success drop when two agents collaborate, and claims contract-first decomposition cuts semantic conflicts by 60–70% ([Vaughan](https://codex.danielvaughan.com/2026/06/06/multi-agent-coordination-problem-concurrent-agents-semantic-conflicts-codex-cli/)). Those figures come from studies not independently verified here, so treat them as directional. A 2026 preprint names "spec-code drift" as the core failure and proposes a drift gate as a merge requirement ([arXiv 2606.27045](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2606.27045)).

Shared instructions belong in **AGENTS.md**, because Codex never reads CLAUDE.md. Codex concatenates AGENTS.md files from the git root down to the working directory, closer files last and therefore higher priority, and stops adding files at a 32 KiB default cap ([Codex AGENTS.md guide](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/agent-configuration/agents-md)). Claude Code reads AGENTS.md natively from v2.1.277, but **by default only when no CLAUDE.md exists**, and even a personal CLAUDE.local.md switches that off. The documented pattern is a CLAUDE.md that begins with `@AGENTS.md` and then adds Claude-only rules, which never double-loads ([Claude Code memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)). Keep each file under about 200 lines, because longer files "reduce adherence," and contradictory rules lead Claude to pick one arbitrarily ([memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)).

The repo layout should give each agent exclusive territory. The proposed structure puts `contracts/openapi.yaml` at the root as the source of truth, `packages/api-client/` generated from it and never hand-edited, `apps/api/` (FastAPI) owned by Claude Code, `apps/web/` (Next.js) and `apps/extension/` (MV3) owned by Codex, each with its own nested AGENTS.md, and an append-only `docs/HANDOFF.md` for contract change requests and open questions. The contract comes first. Claude Code drafts the OpenAPI 3.1 spec and a human freezes it. A single generator (openapi-typescript plus openapi-fetch is the lightest; Hey API is FastAPI's documented default) produces the typed client ([FastAPI: generating SDKs](https://fastapi.tiangolo.com/advanced/generate-clients/); [openapi-typescript](https://openapi-ts.dev/introduction)). Codex builds against `prism mock contracts/openapi.yaml` before any backend exists ([Prism](https://github.com/stoplightio/prism)), or against MSW handlers that Orval can generate from the same spec ([Orval MSW guide](https://orval.dev/guides/msw)). FastAPI publishes its own `/openapi.json` for free, so the drift check is a diff of that output against the frozen contract plus `prism proxy` validating real traffic.

Enforce ownership with tooling, because Markdown is advisory. Claude Code's docs are explicit that CLAUDE.md is "not enforced configuration" and that hooks and permissions are the deterministic layer ([memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory); [best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)). During the backend phase, add `"deny": ["Edit(/apps/web/**)", "Edit(/apps/extension/**)", "Read(./.env)"]` to `.claude/settings.json`. Deny rules are evaluated before allow rules, and path rules apply to Edit and Read but not Write ([permissions](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permissions)). Run each agent in its own worktree: `claude --worktree backend` creates `.claude/worktrees/backend/` on its own branch, and `.worktreeinclude` copies gitignored `.env` files in ([Claude Code worktrees](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/worktrees)). Codex can run from a plain `git worktree add ../careerlens-web -b frontend/codex` checkout under its default `workspace-write` sandbox with `on-request` approvals and network off ([Codex approvals](https://learn.chatgpt.com/codex/agent-approvals-security)). Merge small branches to main often and rebase the other, and assign the root lockfile to one owner, because it is the classic conflict point ([Vaughan](https://codex.danielvaughan.com/2026/06/06/multi-agent-coordination-problem-concurrent-agents-semantic-conflicts-codex-cli/)).

The third pass should be a distinct role, not more feature work. Remove the deny rules, start Claude Code in plan mode (`claude --permission-mode plan`), have it read HANDOFF.md and both branch diffs, then run the checks: backend tests, the OpenAPI drift diff, typecheck, `next build`, and an unpacked extension load. Close with `/code-review` in a fresh subagent, told to report only correctness and requirement gaps, since reviewers asked to find problems over-report ([best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)). A Stop hook can refuse to end the session until the checks pass.

Check one constraint before planning around Codex. As of October 2026, the ChatGPT Free plan's Codex access is limited to the desktop app with a small model "subject to rollout," while CLI, IDE and cloud access start at Plus ($20/month) or require an API key ([Codex pricing](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/pricing)). The same page also lists inconsistent model names, so confirm the model in-app with `/model` before writing it into any doc.

## Conclusion

The team's own additions (JD scraping, tailored ATS resumes, application tracking) are the part of CareerLens most likely to make it look like a clone, because Simplify, Teal and Huntr already give them away. Treat the extension as the delivery surface for the verification engine, not a feature set to compete on: an evidence-weighted match score and a tailoring step that refuses to add claims the evidence does not support. Since HackerRank has open-sourced the obvious baseline under MIT, the stronger move is to borrow its authorship-threshold and originality ideas openly and show judges what it lacks: per-claim links, design evidence and the placement-cell cohort view.

The free stack forces design decisions that also improve the product. Unpublished, shrinking Gemini quotas push the team toward caching and a provider-agnostic structured-output wrapper. Closed LinkedIn and Behance APIs push it toward user-supplied, consented evidence. The free-tier data-use terms push it toward PII stripping. Each of these can be presented in the pitch as a privacy and robustness choice. The biggest unknown is not technical: nobody has shown that proof-of-work scores predict placement outcomes. A batch pilot with VIT Chennai's placement data, comparing evidence scores against offers, would be the most persuasive next step after the hackathon.
